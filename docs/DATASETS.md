# Dataset documentation (datasheets)

Per-dataset provenance, licensing, preprocessing and known caveats for every family in
UpliftBench. **No dataset is redistributed in this repository**: each loader downloads from
the original source at first use and caches under `data/`, so the upstream terms apply
unchanged. Where a distributor states no explicit licence, that is recorded as such rather
than guessed — check the source URL before any redistribution or commercial use.

Machine-readable counterparts of the "Outcome" and "Reference objective" rows are available
at runtime as `ds.meta.outcome_type` and `ds.meta.has_ground_truth_effect`.

| Family | Instances | Outcome | Reference objective | Source |
|--------|-----------|---------|---------------------|--------|
| Synthetic | 1 | binary | per-unit effects (√PEHE) | generated in-repo |
| IHDP | 10 used of 100 | **continuous** | per-unit effects (√PEHE) | fredjo.com (Shalit et al.) |
| Jobs | 10 splits | binary | RCT-estimated policy risk | fredjo.com (Shalit et al.) |

## Synthetic (in-repo generator)

* **Provenance.** Generated by `src/uplift_bench/data/synthetic.py`; no external data, no
  human subjects. Seeded, so it regenerates deterministically.
* **Licence.** MIT, with this repository.
* **Outcome.** **Binary** by configuration (`configs/dataset/synthetic.yaml` sets
  `binary_outcome: true`). It is therefore grouped with the *binary* families in every
  panel, table and figure — it is **not** part of the continuous-outcome evidence for F1.
  A continuous variant exists (`binary_outcome=False`) and is used only in controlled probes.
* **Construction.** 8 Gaussian covariates, treatment assigned with known constant
  propensity 0.5, latent CATE a linear function of covariates, outcome drawn from a
  clipped Bernoulli whose rate shifts with the treatment effect. Per-unit effects are
  attached, so √PEHE is exact.
* **Caveats.** A synthetic DGP cannot establish external validity; it is used for
  mechanism probes and as a binary control, never as standalone evidence.

## IHDP (semi-synthetic; the continuous-outcome family)

* **Provenance.** Covariates from the Infant Health and Development Program, a randomized
  intervention for low-birth-weight infants; potential outcomes are **simulated** in the
  standard setup of Hill (2011), in the 100-realization form distributed by Shalit et al.
  (2017). Downloaded from `https://www.fredjo.com/files/ihdp_npci_1-100.{train,test}.npz`.
* **Licence.** The distributor states no explicit licence for these `.npz` files; they are
  a de-facto community benchmark used under academic-research norms. Not redistributed
  here. Confirm terms with the distributor before redistribution or commercial use.
* **Outcome.** **Continuous** (simulated response surface). This is the only continuous
  family in the primary panel, and therefore the entire identified basis for F1.
* **Preprocessing.** 25 covariates; the standard 672-unit train portion of the 747-unit
  release; treatment fraction 0.19; median imputation fitted on the training fold only.
  Propensity is **estimated** per fold (the covariate–treatment relation is confounded by
  construction), not known.
* **Caveats.** Effects are simulated, so "ground truth" means ground truth *of the
  simulation*, not of the original trial. Outcomes are heavy-tailed and vary substantially
  across realizations (per-realization excess kurtosis −0.9 to 27), which is why F1 is
  reported over all 100 realizations rather than a single draw.

## Jobs (LaLonde + PSID; the policy-objective family)

* **Provenance.** The LaLonde (1986) job-training experiment combined with PSID
  observational controls, in the 10 re-split form of Shalit et al. (2017). Downloaded from
  `https://www.fredjo.com/files/jobs_DW_bin.new.10.{train,test}.npz`.
* **Licence.** As IHDP: no explicit licence stated by the distributor; academic-research
  use; not redistributed here.
* **Outcome.** **Binary** (employment).
* **Reference objective.** An **RCT-estimated reference policy risk**
  `1 − Ê[Y(π)]` computed by IPW on the randomized experimental subset (Shalit et al.,
  2017). This is **not** an oracle optimal policy and Jobs has **no per-unit effects**:
  `meta.has_ground_truth_effect` is `False`, and the `ite` frame carries only the
  `experimental` flag that identifies the randomized subset.
* **Caveats.** The dataset mixes an experimental arm with observational controls, so the
  treated/control contrast conflates uplift with selection outside the experimental subset.
  The 10 "instances" are **overlapping re-splits of one sample** (mean pairwise test-unit
  Jaccard 0.11), not independent datasets — which is why the paper treats F2's empirical
  half as a within-sample case study.

## Hillstrom (marketing RCT)

* **Provenance.** Kevin Hillstrom's MineThatData E-Mail Analytics And Data Mining
  Challenge (2008), released publicly for analysis. Downloaded from
  `https://www.minethatdata.com/Kevin_Hillstrom_MineThatData_E-MailAnalytics_DataMiningChallenge_2008.03.20.csv`.
* **Licence.** Published for public analysis by the author; no formal licence text
  accompanies the CSV. Not redistributed here.
* **Outcome.** **Binary** (`visit` by default; `conversion` also available).
* **Preprocessing / caveats.** The original experiment has **three arms** (men's email,
  women's email, no email). The loader collapses it to a binary treatment by default
  (`treatment_arm="any"`: either email vs none), which is why the treatment fraction is
  0.667. Used in full (64,000 rows). A three-arm analysis is out of scope for this
  benchmark's binary-treatment interface.

## Lenta, X5, MegaFon (marketing RCTs via scikit-uplift)

* **Provenance.** Fetched through `scikit-uplift`'s dataset API
  (`sklift.datasets.fetch_lenta / fetch_x5 / fetch_megafon`), which downloads each from its
  own upstream host. Lenta: retail promotion campaign. X5: X5 Retail Group "RetailHero"
  uplift competition. MegaFon: MegaFon uplift competition.
* **Licence.** Each dataset carries the terms of its own publisher/competition, which are
  **not** the MIT licence of `scikit-uplift` (the access library) nor of this repository.
  Competition data is typically restricted to research/non-commercial use. Nothing is
  redistributed here; consult the upstream terms via the scikit-uplift dataset pages before
  any reuse.
* **Outcome.** **Binary** in all three.
* **Preprocessing.** Each is capped at **10,000 rows by documented stratified subsampling**
  (stratified on treatment × outcome, fixed seed) to fit the released compute budget; full
  sizes are ~687K / ~200K / ~600K. Marketing leaderboards must therefore be read as
  subsample-scoped, and no headline finding rests on them.
* **Caveats.** No counterfactual and no per-unit effects, so only proxy metrics (Qini/AUUC)
  are computable — which is precisely the situation the paper warns about. Some
  model × dataset cells time out and are reported as such, not imputed.

## Revenue-Synthetic (auxiliary, in-repo generator)

* **Provenance / licence.** Generated in-repo; MIT.
* **Outcome.** **Continuous**, heavy-tailed (revenue-like), with per-unit effects.
* **Role.** Used only for the F1 extension and controlled probes, not in the 25 primary
  instances.

## ACIC 2016 (auxiliary; independent boundary-case family)

* **Provenance.** The 2016 Atlantic Causal Inference Competition setup (Dorie et al.,
  2019): **real** Collaborative Perinatal Project covariates (n=4,802) with simulated
  response surfaces, generated by the official competition package.
* **Licence.** Covariates and generators are distributed by the competition organizers
  under their own terms; the simulation is regenerated deterministically here rather than
  redistributed.
* **Outcome.** **Continuous**, but **mild-tailed** (excess kurtosis ≈0.4) — which is why it
  serves as the boundary case where F1 is *not* observed.
* **Scope.** 18 instances (9 heterogeneity × overlap settings × 2 replicates); an auxiliary
  family, not one of the seven primary ones.

## Criteo (specified in the harness, no results in v1)

* **Provenance.** Criteo Uplift Prediction dataset (~14M rows),
  `http://go.criteo.net/criteo-research-uplift-v2.1.csv.gz`.
* **Licence.** Criteo Research's own dataset terms apply; consult the download page.
* **Status.** A versioned loader, documented 1M/5M subsample tiers, config and
  cluster-scale timeout budgets exist, but **no Criteo results are reported** in v1. The
  exclusion is computational, not methodological, and is disclosed in the paper's
  Limitations.

## Ethics and responsible-use notes

* All data are public and de-identified by their publishers; **no new personal data is
  collected or released** by this project.
* Two families concern consequential human outcomes (IHDP: infant health; Jobs: employment
  programme eligibility). They are used here strictly as *evaluation* benchmarks for
  metric behaviour — no model trained here is intended for deployment decisions about
  people, and the IHDP outcomes are simulated.
* Uplift targeting allocates real resources (marketing spend, programme access). A
  mis-specified evaluation metric misdirects them, which is the risk this benchmark exists
  to reduce; conversely, uplift models can amplify inequities when treatment access, cost
  or outcomes differ across groups. UpliftBench does **not** evaluate subgroup fairness or
  treatment burden, so its results do not validate high-stakes deployment.
